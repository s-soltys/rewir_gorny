# Working plot outline — v0.1

**Status: PROVISIONAL.** This is a proposed causal structure from the accumulated discussion, not approved canon or final dialogue. Keep the original mystery open; make human actions, clues, and consequences legible.

## Story promise

A homecoming story becomes a factional investigation into an impossible place. The player can understand why each group acts as it does, but no group—and no reveal—fully explains the anomaly.

## Provisional logline

A university student returns to Piła in the 1990s and finds the friends they left behind splitting into rival survival strategies around a copper-linked distortion of memory and place. Every explanation fits some evidence; every strategy protects someone and puts someone else at risk. The exact year is still PROVISIONAL.

## Act I — Return and first disturbance

1. **Homecoming:** The protagonist returns to the family flat in Osiedle Górne after time away. The immediate reason is OPEN. Show the gap between the university city and home through routines and objects, not a history lecture.
2. **The kiosk:** An age-uncertain figure known around the kiosk as the Weirdo mentions “the copper” and the hum. A strange coin from the family flat may connect the scene to prior events; its meaning is not established. The protagonist may ask, show it, or leave.
3. **The clearing:** The protagonist finds the Gateway Friend and the cable-burning group. The gathering begins as scrap work and company. A disturbance is witnessed differently by different people.
4. **Commitment:** The protagonist chooses whom to help first. A personal request or consequence binds them to follow the evidence; the specific objective is OPEN.

## Act II — Factions and recontextualization

5. **Three partial maps:** The protagonist encounters the Burners, a possible Cordon/Record-Keeping group, and the Scrap Network. Each identifies real harms and misreads or withholds other evidence.
6. **Costly intervention:** Closing a route, moving cable, marking a site, or sharing a record helps one group and harms another. The action should change trust, safety, or a relationship.
7. **Midpoint candidate:** A map, record, or witness pattern suggests the cable fires did not create the anomaly. Similar spatial inconsistencies may predate the present group and be recorded around the airfield. The existence, provenance, and reliability of this evidence are OPEN; research real airfield history before making historical claims.
8. **Relationship reversal:** The Gateway Friend's effort to protect the group becomes controlling. Their fear is understandable, but the protagonist must decide whether loyalty means staying, resisting, or leaving.

## Act III — Connected clues, irreducible unknown

9. **Pattern, not explanation:** The coin, cables, hum, records, and contradictory routes appear to fit one pattern. This clarifies what people have been responding to without revealing an entity's intent or a complete physical mechanism.
10. **Strategies collide:** The factions act on their beliefs at once. Their choices—not a hidden mastermind—create the immediate crisis.
11. **Climax:** The protagonist makes a costly choice about the Gateway Friend, the group, and whether to expose or suppress the records. Exact options and outcomes remain OPEN.
12. **Aftermath:** Resolve one human question and show a changed local situation. Leave the larger anomaly unsettled. The final clue should revise an earlier interpretation, not nullify the evidence that led there.

## Candidate reveal architecture

- **Surface:** Is this contamination, a spatial fault, or altered perception?
- **Human:** Who knew what, who profited, and why did they choose their response?
- **Historical reframe:** The pattern appears older than the current cable fires; records show observation or avoidance, not understanding.
- **Remainder:** The anomaly itself stays unknowable.

See `revelation-map.md` for candidate clues. These are structural proposals, not confirmed lore.

## Prototype cut line

For a first playable slice, use the homecoming, kiosk, clearing, and one follow-up scene that tests the competing explanations. Do not build the whole three-act story or all faction branches into the first prototype.
