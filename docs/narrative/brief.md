# Narrative brief — Rewir Górny

**Status:** working brief. Direct user-established items are marked CANON; all extrapolations remain PROVISIONAL.

## CANON: creative promise

- **Format:** interactive narrative / shifting-reality fiction, with a game to follow after the world and plot are defined.
- **Place/time:** Piła's Osiedle Górne in 1990s Poland (CANON); 1996 is a working date inherited from prototype materials, still PROVISIONAL.
- **Social ground:** post-communist transition; *wielka płyta*, market stalls, secondhand Western goods, pine forest and the old airfield at the edge of the neighborhood.
- **Cable practice:** youth burn discarded industrial copper cable for scrap and as a social gathering. It is not merely an act of desperate poverty.
- **Anomaly:** the original premise says burning the late-era industrial cable releases a vapor that alters perception of time and space; it is associated with slow, creeping synesthesia and **permanent amplification** of individual psychological traits. The mechanism and how permanence appears in play remain OPEN.
- **Protagonist concept:** an early-twenties local left for university in a larger city and returns to Piła; distance changes how they experience the phenomenon.
- **Tone:** grounded, nostalgic, transitional, grimdark, and increasingly uncanny. The anomaly is less religious than indifferent and unknowable.

## Reference points, not templates

The user initially explored a traditional novel and a “return to a place you swore you'd never see again” story spine in an invented world. That was superseded by the interactive narrative set in real Piła. The chosen tonal palette is **cracked modernity**: *Fargo* plus a loose *Disco Elysium* influence, with *Fallout/Mad Max*, *Planescape: Torment*, *The Three-Body Problem*, and grounded crime and non-crime dramas including *Scarface* and *Goodfellas*. *Disco Elysium* is an influence on tone/writing/art, not a template. The user later added *Attack on Titan* as a reference for plausible faction motives, connected clues, and major recontextualizing reveals—not copied lore or plot.

## PROVISIONAL working logline

When a university student returns to 1996 Piła, the friends they left behind are splitting into rival survival strategies around a copper-linked distortion of memory and place. Each group has real reasons for what it does—and each explanation fits only part of the evidence.

## PROVISIONAL dramatic question

Can the protagonist stay loyal to someone they left behind without becoming complicit in the damage that person causes while trying to keep the group together?

## Story and world constraints

- The horror should remain **unfathomable**: show effects and human interpretations, but do not give the anomaly a confirmed motive or complete scientific/theological explanation.
- Make causal links, character choices, and evidence fair and legible even when the underlying force is not.
- The protagonist's choices should focus on how they relate to changing friends. They cannot stop the decade's transition or simply “solve” the anomaly.
- Keep the scale local and emotionally specific. The prototype need not explain all of Poland, the military, or the universe.
- Grimdark comes from scarcity, bad incentives, irreversible losses, and inadequate choices—not from indiscriminate cruelty or surface-level bleakness.
- Faction positions must be internally coherent: each should be right about something, wrong about something, and capable of harming others.

## PROVISIONAL prototype scope

A short opening sequence across the family flat, street/kiosk, forest clearing, and—if needed for the first major reframe—the airfield. It should establish the return, the Gateway Friend, competing interpretations, and one clue that changes the apparent cause. Full game scope, branching scale, and ending count remain OPEN.

## Not decided yet

The exact reason for this particular return; the protagonist's concrete first objective; faction membership and names; the hidden history of the airfield; whether cable-burning causes, reveals, or merely correlates with the anomaly; the final twist; and the ending choice.
